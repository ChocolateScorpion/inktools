iPhone Live Photos: what they are, how they work, and how to convert them

Live Photos are one of those iPhone features that feel like a small magic trick. Press the shutter and your phone doesn't just freeze a single frame — it captures a brief window of real life, a moment before and after the click. Whether you've used them for years or are just discovering the feature, there's quite a bit more to Live Photos than the animated preview suggests. This guide covers exactly what they are, how they're stored, how to share them without losing the motion, and how to convert them to video when you need to post them somewhere that doesn't support the format.

What is a Live Photo?

When you take a Live Photo on iPhone, the camera captures 1.5 seconds of video and audio before you press the shutter and 1.5 seconds after — a total of 3 seconds of motion wrapped around your still image. The still frame itself is a full-resolution HEIC photo. The motion component is a short MOV video file. Together they make up a Live Photo, and in the Photos app they appear as a single item.

The feature was introduced with the iPhone 6s in 2015. Apple's pitch at the time was that it transformed a static memory into something closer to a scene from a moving newspaper — a photo that breathes. In practice, it makes photos feel warmer and more immediate, especially for portraits of children, pets, and candid moments where the fraction of a second before and after the click often contains something you'd otherwise lose entirely. That half-second of someone's laugh winding down, the anticipation before the birthday candles are blown out — Live Photos catch both.

How Live Photos are stored

Under the hood, a Live Photo is always a pair of files that travel together:

  • A HEIC still image — the main photo at full resolution, typically 12–48MP depending on your iPhone model.
  • A MOV video file — the 3-second motion clip recorded at 30fps, with audio.

Both files share the same filename (apart from the extension) and are linked by metadata so iOS knows they belong together. This is why they appear as a single item in Photos, but if you export them manually or use certain apps, you'll see both files separately.

The HEIC format (High Efficiency Image Container) is Apple's preferred still format, offering roughly 2× better compression than JPG at comparable quality. It's the default on all iPhones since iOS 11 and handles HDR, depth maps (for Portrait mode), and the embedded Live Photo motion component all within the same container. The combined size of a Live Photo is noticeably larger than a regular still — a typical shot from a recent iPhone runs 4–7MB versus 2–3MB for a still. If you shoot hundreds of photos per month, that difference accumulates into gigabytes quickly.

Why the larger size matters: A camera roll of 5,000 Live Photos can run 10–15GB larger than the same 5,000 stills. If storage is tight, selectively disabling Live for everyday shots — food, screenshots, quick notes — saves meaningful space.

How to view Live Photos on different devices

The Live Photo experience varies significantly depending on where you're viewing it:

  • iPhone: Press and hold on the photo in the Photos app. The still frame animates into motion with sound. On modern iPhones, a long press triggers it. You can also tap the Live badge at the top of the photo to toggle it on or off.
  • Mac: In the Photos app, hover your cursor over a Live Photo thumbnail and it plays. In Quick Look (spacebar in Finder), click the Live button or hold the mouse button to play the motion.
  • iPad: Same as iPhone — press and hold to view the motion.
  • Windows: No native support. Windows Photos and File Explorer treat Live Photos as regular stills. The MOV file may appear separately if you import via File Explorer rather than through iCloud.
  • Android: The standard gallery treats a Live Photo as a still. However, Google Photos on Android displays synced iPhone Live Photos as Motion Photos (Google's equivalent), so recipients using Google Photos will see the animation.
  • iCloud.com: Works well. Live Photos animate when you click them in the Photos web app and when shared via iCloud Photo Links.
  • WhatsApp: Strips the Live component entirely. The recipient gets a static still regardless of settings. There is no way to send the motion component through WhatsApp — you need to convert to video first.
  • Google Photos (iOS): Preserves the Live Photo when you upload from iPhone. From Google Photos, you can share the motion as an animated GIF.

Live Photo effects

The Photos app lets you apply four effects to Live Photos, accessible by swiping up on a Live Photo in full-screen view or tapping the effects button at the bottom of the screen:

  • Live (the default): Shows the natural 3 seconds of motion when you press and hold.
  • Loop: Takes the motion clip and loops it continuously, turning your Live Photo into something closer to a cinemagraph — a looping image that reads as a photo but moves like a video.
  • Bounce: Plays the clip forwards, then reverses it back, creating the "boomerang" effect. Particularly effective for action shots where the motion looks interesting in both directions.
  • Long Exposure: The most interesting one. It analyzes all frames in the motion clip and blends them into a single composite image, simulating the effect of a DSLR set to a slow shutter speed on a tripod. Running water becomes silk. Waterfalls blur into ribbons. Moving crowds merge into ghosts. Lights trace trails. The result is a computationally generated long exposure photograph — and it works surprisingly well if your subject involves continuous motion and you held the phone reasonably steady during the shot.

All effects are applied non-destructively. You can always revert to the original Live setting.

How to turn off Live Photos

Live Photos add storage weight and don't transmit the motion component through every sharing channel. Here's how to control them:

  • Per-shot: Tap the yellow Live Photos icon (the circle with radiating lines) in the Camera app's top control bar. It grays out, indicating Live is off for the next shot. It resets to on when you close and reopen the Camera app.
  • Persistently between sessions: Go to Settings → Camera → Preserve Settings and toggle on "Live Photo". This remembers your last-used Live setting between Camera sessions, so if you turned it off, it stays off until you turn it back on.
  • Turn off on existing photos: Open a Live Photo, tap the Live badge in the top-left corner, and select "Off". This doesn't delete the motion data — it just tells Photos not to animate it. You can re-enable it at any time.

Sharing Live Photos

Getting the animation to survive the sharing process is trickier than it should be. Here's what preserves the motion and what strips it:

  • AirDrop: Fully preserves the Live component. Both files transfer together and the recipient's iPhone recognizes the pair as a Live Photo.
  • iMessage: Live Photos animate in iMessage between iPhones. The motion is preserved. On Android recipients, it arrives as a still.
  • Email (from iOS Mail): Sends only the still image. To send both files, export the Live Photo to Files first, then attach both the HEIC and MOV manually.
  • Instagram: Posts the still frame only. You need to convert to video and upload as a Reel or video post to share the motion.
  • WhatsApp: Still image only, as noted above. No workaround within WhatsApp itself.
  • Facebook: Strips the motion on mobile. Some browsers show a brief animation in the upload preview, but the posted result is a still.
  • Google Photos: Preserves the Live Photo on upload from iPhone. The best cloud platform for Live Photo fidelity outside of iCloud.

Live Photos and storage

Because each Live Photo is roughly twice the size of an equivalent still, they accumulate quickly on a full camera roll. Here are practical strategies for managing the storage impact:

Check your usage: Go to Settings → General → iPhone Storage → Photos to see how much space your photo library is using in total. There's no built-in breakdown of Live versus still, but third-party apps like Gemini Photos can scan for Live Photos specifically.

iCloud Optimize Storage: If you have iCloud Photos enabled, turning on "Optimize iPhone Storage" in Settings → Photos keeps lower-resolution proxies on the device while storing full-resolution versions (including the motion component) in iCloud. This is the most seamless way to reclaim local space without permanently losing any Live Photo data.

Be selective: Reserve Live Photos for moments where motion genuinely adds value — birthday candles, a child's first steps, waves hitting shore, a pet mid-run. For food photos, landscapes, and quick reference shots, turn Live off. You'll save significant space and the resulting stills are often just as useful.

Disable Live on existing photos in bulk: Open Photos, select a group of Live Photos, tap the three-dot menu, and choose "Edit". From there you can disable the Live component across the selection. The motion data remains stored but is no longer active, reducing the effective footprint until you decide to delete the MOV files entirely.

Creative uses of Live Photos

Beyond the obvious, Live Photos have some compelling creative applications that most people never discover:

Cinemagraphs: A cinemagraph is a still photograph with a single, repeated element of motion — a candle flame flickering, steam rising from a cup, a flag waving in the wind while the rest of the scene is frozen. Shoot with the static elements of the frame as still as possible, apply the Loop effect, save as video, and you have a cinemagraph. Share to Instagram or Twitter as a looping video clip and it draws significantly more attention than a standard still.

GIFs from Live Photos: Google Photos lets you convert a Live Photo to an animated GIF via the "Create" menu. Several third-party apps including GIPHY Cam and Lively offer the same conversion with more control over frame rate and loop count. The resulting GIF quality is limited by the 3-second source clip, but for messaging and web embedding it's usually perfectly usable.

Choosing the best frame: If someone is mid-jump or mid-pitch when you take a Live Photo, you can scrub through the frames in third-party apps or in Photos' editing view to find the perfect frozen moment — effectively giving you multiple frames to choose from rather than a single shutter capture.

Frequently asked questions

Do Live Photos use more storage?
Yes, roughly twice as much as an equivalent still. A Live Photo consists of both a full-resolution HEIC still image and a 3-second MOV video clip. A typical Live Photo from a recent iPhone runs 4–7MB versus 2–3MB for a still. Over thousands of photos, this difference becomes substantial — a camera roll of 5,000 Live Photos can easily run 10–15GB larger than the same number of stills.
Can I send a Live Photo on WhatsApp?
Not with the motion intact. WhatsApp strips the Live component on send and delivers only the still image to the recipient. To share the motion, convert the Live Photo to MP4 first — either through Photos → Share → "Save as Video", or using a browser-based converter at inktools.tech/heic-to-mp4/ — and then send the video file through WhatsApp normally.
How do I convert a Live Photo to a video?
The quickest method on iPhone: open the Live Photo in Photos, tap the Share button (the box with an arrow), scroll down, and tap "Save as Video". This saves the 3-second motion clip as an MP4 to your camera roll. For browser-based conversion from a desktop or for converting the HEIC file directly, inktools.tech/heic-to-mp4/ handles the process in seconds without requiring any app installation or account sign-up.
Can Android users see Live Photos?
Android devices don't natively support the Live Photo format, so standard Android gallery apps display only the still frame. However, Google Photos on Android does display synced iPhone Live Photos as Motion Photos — Google's own equivalent format — so recipients who use Google Photos as their default gallery will see the animation play when they tap the image.
How do I make a GIF from a Live Photo?
Google Photos is the easiest path: open the Live Photo in Google Photos, tap the three-dot menu, and look for the "Create" option or "Animation". This generates a looping animated GIF from the motion clip. Third-party apps including Lively and GIPHY Cam offer the same conversion with additional controls over frame rate, duration, and loop count. The resulting GIF reflects the quality and duration of the original 3-second Live Photo clip.
Does disabling Live Photos affect still image quality?
No. Turning off Live Photos has no effect on the quality of the still image captured. You get the same full-resolution HEIC photo regardless — Live Photos simply adds the short MOV motion video alongside the still. The still frame is identical whether Live is enabled or not, shot at the same resolution, dynamic range, and quality as a regular photo taken in the same conditions.
